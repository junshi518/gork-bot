When a tuck fails

Kinusaiga Troubleshooting for a First Piece

What you see on the board, why it happened, and what to try before you throw the foam out.

Most first-piece failures are one of three cuts: the groove is too shallow, the foam is crushed, or the cloth was trimmed to its outline before it was tucked. Match the symptom below, try the fix once, and look across the board at a low angle before you try a second time. Pushing harder is not a fix.

Use the flat end of the seam ripper for every repair. Flat back. The forked point cuts the cloth. A ball tip is not the tool this method uses. If you have been tucking with either of those, switch ends before you rescore.

The groove is too shallow

You see the edge spring back as soon as the tool lifts. Or you see a ridge of cloth sitting on the line because you kept pushing into a scratch.

  1. Stop forcing that line. Lift the cloth edge back out with the flat end. Slide under the fold. Do not stab through the face.
  2. Score the same line again until the slot is about 1/4 inch deep and foam still remains under it. One clean pass with a sharp blade. Not a row of scratches.
  3. Pick foam dust out of the groove. Dust holds the cloth up.
  4. Tuck again in short pushes. The edge should stay down when you lift the tool. If it does, trim only the tail that will not fit.

If the second score comes out the back of the board, the sheet is too thin. That is display foam, or a board well under 1 inch. Stop. A new groove will not grow more thickness. The comparison is on Foam and supplies.

An edge pops out later

It stayed down while you worked, then lifted after you moved to the next shape, or after you carried the board.

  • The slot was deep enough for a moment and too shallow for the thickness of this cotton. Rescore toward 1/4 inch and tuck again.
  • The margin was short, so almost no cloth is in the channel. If you still have a tail, tuck that tail. If you already trimmed it flush, this shape needs a new piece of cloth with about 1/2 inch to spare. See cutting.
  • The walls were crushed, so the slot closed. Go to the crushed-foam section. Do not rescore a mashed line with the same dull blade.
  • You used the forked point or a ball tip, and the groove is now wider than the cloth can fill. Switch to the flat end. If a neighboring piece still has spare cloth, tuck that spare into the torn slot. If the tear is too wide, that stretch will not hold. The next board starts with the flat end.

Wrinkles

A wrinkle in the field is not the same as a ridge on the groove. The ridge is cloth that never entered the slot. The wrinkle is cloth that is stuck to the face in the wrong place.

  1. If you have not tucked this shape yet, peel it up now, while the adhesive is still the main hold. Smooth from the center outward and press again. Then tuck.
  2. If the wrinkle runs into a groove, the tuck was uneven. Lift that edge with the flat end, ease the extra cloth back onto the face, and feed a smaller bite.
  3. If every piece wrinkles the same way, the cloth was probably cut on the bias. It stretches as you push. Recut on the straight grain, up and down the board, as on the fabric page.
  4. If the adhesive has set and the wrinkle will not lift without tearing the cloth, replace that one shape. Do not soak the board. Water and the adhesive are not a fix.

A hard crease you forgot to press will look like a wrinkle and will not tuck out. Replace the piece, or live with the crease if it is in the background and you can stand it. Press the next piece before it touches the board.

Corners

Two grooves meet. The cloth wants to make a lump, or the foam wants to tear out a chunk.

  1. Do not dig a hole at the intersection with the knife. The two slots only need to meet.
  2. Tuck one side until the cloth reaches the corner. Tuck the other side the same way.
  3. If a lump will not enter, snip into the margin toward the corner. Stop before the blade reaches the groove. Tuck the two small flaps with the flat end.
  4. If you snipped past the line, you have a hole and no cloth to cover it. A new piece for that shape is the repair, with the snip kept in the margin this time.
  5. If the corner is a needle tip, it will keep failing. Round that corner on the next drawing. The first pattern uses blobs for this reason. You can widen a sharp point on this board only by scoring a slightly rounder line beside it, and only if you still have spare cloth. Otherwise finish the rest of the leaf and treat that point as the part you will not repeat.

Adhesive showing

Adhesive belongs on the face, under the cloth. It should not flash in the picture, and it should not fill the groove.

  • A shiny patch in a lobe means the cloth does not cover the field, or you peeled the liner past the groove. Cover it with a new piece that has a 1/2 inch margin. Do not color over the adhesive.
  • A hairline of adhesive in a shared groove means the two edges do not meet. Retuck both so they sit side by side in the slot. The tuck order is background first, then the lobe beside it.
  • Adhesive or tape down in the channel gums the tuck. Pick it out with the flat end before you push cloth in. Do not add more.
  • Dust on open adhesive is stuck. If the cloth just went down and is not tucked, lift it, throw that piece away, and peel only the next shape when you are ready to cover it. Specks you pressed into the background will stay.

Crushed foam

The walls of the groove are mashed together. Cloth sits on top because there is no slot. The cut looks dusty or torn. This happens when the blade is dull or you lean on it.

  1. Change the blade. Test it on the bare margin. You want a slot, not a trench with collapsed lips.
  2. Do not lean harder on the dull blade. That is what crushed the foam.
  3. If a short stretch is mashed, you may be able to score a fresh slot immediately beside it, inside the same 1/2 inch of spare cloth. Shift the line only if the leaf still reads. Then tuck into the new slot.
  4. If the crush runs the length of a lobe, that shape will not hold. Start a new board. Keep this one as the test for what a dull blade does, so the next sheet gets a sharp one from the first line.

The fabric was cut to the finished shape too soon

The piece matches the drawing. The groove is empty. There is nothing to push in. Forcing the raw edge only frays it on top of the line.

Cut a new piece about 1/2 inch larger than the shape on every side. Peel any liner that is still covered, or lay the new cloth on the adhesive that is already exposed if it is still clean. Press the middle. Tuck. Trim the tail after it stays down. The old, too-small piece cannot be stretched into a margin.

Other first-piece problems

The last side bunches

The border has too much cloth in it. Back the flat end out, pull a little cloth up, trim a sliver, and tuck a shorter bite. The last side is the border groove, after the stem is already in. If you are bunching the stem, you are feeding too long a bite into a narrow piece. Move a finger-width at a time.

The middle of the background bubbles

The tuck does not hold the field. The liner for that shape is still on, or the tape never reached the center. Peel and press the middle before you redo the border.

Foam crumbs in the groove

Blow or pick them out before you tuck. A crumb props the cloth up and looks like a shallow score.

The knife went through the back

Stop scoring that sheet. Patching the back will not give the face a 1/4 inch slot with foam under it. Use about 1 inch of XPS for the next board.

What you need if you start over

A new board is the right call when the foam is crushed along a whole line, cut through the back, or too thin to deepen. You do not need new cloth if the cotton was only trimmed too small on one shape. You do need a sharp blade either way.

  • XPS about 1 inch thick, if this sheet is finished.
  • A double-sided sheet or tape for the face, kept out of the groove.
  • A new blade, and the flat end of the seam ripper.
  • Cotton with about 1/2 inch to spare, if the old pieces were cut to the outline.
