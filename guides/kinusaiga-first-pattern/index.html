Draw the leaf

How to Draw Your First Kinusaiga Pattern

A stem and four lobes on a 6 to 8 inch board, with bare foam around the picture.

Draw this leaf yourself. It is the same motif as the finished-panel schematic: a stem and four rounded lobes on a flat board. Each lobe is at least about 1 inch across. That size tells you whether a tuck went into the foam. A finer drawing hides that, and a copied design from someone else is not part of this practice.

This page draws the leaf and tapes the paper onto the liner. Match the edges. Tape the corners. Leave the paper still and the lines dark. Then stop. The beginners session is where the knife scores those lines.

On this page

  1. Set the size
  2. Draw the leaf
  3. One line where colors meet
  4. What you need
  5. Transfer it to the board
  6. Drawings to avoid on day one

Set the size

  1. Trim the foam to about 6 to 8 inches on the long side if you have not already. The board should be about 1 inch of XPS. A thin display board will not hold the lines you are about to draw.
  2. Cut a piece of paper to the same size as the board. Printer paper is fine. This is the sheet you will tape to the liner. The beginners session scores through it later.
  3. Draw a rectangle about 1 inch in from each edge of the paper. That inner rectangle is the border groove. Outside it, write “margin” in light pencil so you do not fill it with shapes. The margin stays bare. The next page uses it for a test cut, and a later frame covers it.

Draw the leaf

Work inside the border. Keep the pencil light until the shapes look right, then darken only the lines you will cut.

  1. Stem. From the middle of the bottom border, draw a strip about as wide as your index finger, running up to the center of the rectangle. A hairline stem has no cloth to tuck. Keep both sides smooth. A wobble becomes a groove you have to follow with the knife.
  2. Four lobes. Each lobe is a rounded shape, at least about 1 inch across, touching the stem or a neighboring lobe. Put two toward the top of the stem and two lower, so the leaf reads as one plant rather than four islands. Round every corner. A point tears the foam first.
  3. Background. The space left inside the border is one connected field. If a leftover gap is only a sliver, widen a lobe until the gap disappears. Tiny background scraps are as hard as tiny flowers.
  4. Label each area lightly: background, lobe, stem. The tuck order is background, then lobes, then stem, then the border. The numbers on the plan match that order. You will not tuck today unless the session is what you opened this page to support.

If you want a second copy, trace your own pencil drawing. Trace that. Do not go looking for another person’s leaf to download.

One line where colors meet

Where two lobes touch, draw one shared line. Where a lobe meets the background, draw one shared line. Two lines with a gap between them become two grooves and a sliver of foam that collapses.

The border is also one line, the rectangle you already drew. Do not add a second rectangle a hair outside it. Do not draw lines in the margin.

Darken only the lines the knife will follow on the next page. Erase construction marks. A leftover tick becomes a groove you did not want. The paper is a guide. It is not the finished picture.

What you need

  • The foam board, about 6 to 8 inches, with the adhesive sheet’s liner still on the face.
  • Paper cut to the board, a pencil, and an eraser.
  • Tape for the four corners of the paper. This tape holds the pattern. It is not the fabric adhesive.
  • A sharp craft knife, still unused. The beginners session tests the blade in the foam margin. This page does not score.

Cloth can stay in the pile until the lines are scored. Cutting it is the fabric page.

Materials

If you still need a board or a knife, the shop categories are Foam and adhesive, Tools, and Starter kit.

Transfer it to the board

  1. Cover the face with the double-sided sheet first if you have not. Leave the liner on. The paper sits on the liner, not on bare adhesive.
  2. Lay the drawing on the liner so the paper edges match the board edges. The 1 inch margin on the paper is the 1 inch margin on the foam.
  3. Tape the four corners. Press the middle of the paper. It should not bubble or slide. If it moves, tape it again.

Stop here. The drawing is taped down, the edges match the board, and the lines are dark. Go to the beginners session to score. That page cuts each dark line about 1/4 inch deep. Leave the knife alone until you are on that page.

Drawings to avoid on day one

  • Lettering. The counters of letters are tiny grooves, and they crush.
  • A flower made of many pointed petals. Use the four lobes instead.
  • Strips narrower than the stem. The stem is already the narrow piece, and it is tucked last for that reason.
  • A design that runs to the outer edge of the foam. You would lose the test margin and the frame lip would cover the picture.
  • Someone else’s pattern. Draw this leaf. A kit may include its own pattern. Judge that pattern by the same rules: few sharp corners, each lobe at least about 1 inch across, one line where colors meet.
