After the tuck

How to Finish and Frame a Kinusaiga Piece

Check the edges, cover the bare foam with a deep enough frame, and hang the frame.

The piece is a flat board about 1 inch thick. The leaf sits inside a border groove. Outside that groove is bare foam, about 1 inch wide, left there on purpose. Finishing does not mean wrapping cloth around the outside of the board. It means the tucks stay down, the margin stays bare, and a frame lip hides that margin.

Do this only after the board passes the done check on the beginners page. A frame will hide the margin. It will not hide a ridge across a lobe.

Finish the edges

  1. Tip the board and look across it at a low angle. Every groove should read as a line, not a rope of cloth. Retuck a ridge with the flat end of the seam ripper before you think about a frame.
  2. Pick loose threads off the face. Threads buried in a groove can stay.
  3. Walk the border groove, the last side you tucked. The background cloth should sit in that slot. The foam outside it should be bare. If cloth flops over the margin, trim the tail. If the edge pops out, the groove is too shallow. Deepen it to about 1/4 inch and tuck again. Do not glue the edge down on top of the foam.
  4. Check the corners of the leaf. A small tuck of extra cloth is fine if it is inside the groove. A hole where you snipped past the line is not. That repair is on the troubleshooting page, and a frame will not cover the middle of the picture.
  5. Dust the face with a soft brush. Do not wash the board. The adhesive and the tucks are not a washable quilt.

What you need

  • The finished board, about 6 to 8 inches, about 1 inch thick.
  • A frame whose opening matches the board, with a recess deep enough for that 1 inch of foam.
  • Frame points, or tape used on the back of the frame against the back of the foam.
  • A sawtooth hanger or a wire, attached to the frame.
  • The flat end of the seam ripper, in case one border edge still needs a push.

You do not need glass pressing on the cloth. Glass that bears on the tucks can flatten a groove and pop an edge. If the frame includes glass, it needs space in front of the fabric, or leave the glass out.

Materials

A frame is not one of the shop categories. Foam, adhesive, and tools are: Foam and adhesive, Tools, Starter kit. A kit is only useful here if it actually includes a frame that fits this thickness. That check is on Kit vs materials.

Choose and fit a frame

Many photo frames are made for a sheet of paper. Their recess is a few millimeters. Your board is about 1 inch. A shallow frame will not take it. Do not crush the foam to force the board in. Compression closes the grooves, and the edges you just tucked will pop out.

  1. Set the frame face down. Look at the recess, the step the lip makes. The foam has to sit in that step without being squeezed.
  2. If the recess is too shallow, use a deeper frame. A shadow-box style is the usual kind that has room. This page does not name a product. Measure the recess against the board you have.
  3. The opening should show the whole leaf. The lip should land on the bare foam margin and stop before the border groove. If the lip covers a lobe, the frame is too small or the margin you left was too narrow. Do not cut the leaf down. Lean the board unframed until you have a wider opening.
  4. Drop the board in from the back, fabric facing the opening. It should sit without rocking. Rocking means the recess is uneven or something is under one corner. A crumb of foam under a corner is enough. Brush the recess out.
  5. Hold the board with frame points into the frame’s inner edge, or with strips of tape from the back of the frame onto the back of the foam. Tape and points stay on the back. Do not put adhesive on the face. Do not nail through the front.

An uneven margin is a framing problem if the leaf itself is inside the opening. You do not retuck the stem because one side of the foam border is a little wider. You do retuck if the border groove shows as a gap of adhesive or foam inside the picture.

Hang it

Hang the frame. The foam is not a hanging support. A nail through the foam face tears the picture and crushes the board. A nail into the foam edge does not hold the weight.

  1. Attach a sawtooth hanger or a wire to the frame, on the back, above the midpoint so the picture does not tip forward.
  2. Use a picture hook or a nail in the wall, sized for the frame, not for the foam.
  3. After it is up, look along the leaf once more. Carrying the board can shake a shallow tuck loose. If an edge has lifted, take the board out of the frame and fix the groove. Do not push on the cloth through the opening with a point.

If you have no frame yet

Lean the board on a shelf, fabric facing out, with the bottom edge supported so the foam does not bow. Keep it out of a damp window. You can live with it unframed. The tuck is the lesson. The frame is the cover for the margin.

Do not punch a hole for a string. Do not glue a hook to the face. When you do frame it, use the steps above, and keep the lip on the bare foam.
