First practice

Kinusaiga for Beginners: Your First No‑Sew Project

One board, one leaf, from a clear table to a piece you can stop working on.

You are making a flat picture about 6 to 8 inches across. The drawing is a stem and four rounded lobes. The board is about 1 inch of XPS foam. You will score every line about 1/4 inch deep, then tuck cotton into those grooves with the flat end of a seam ripper.

This is not sewing, and it is not carved wood. A couple of hours is a fair plan for this size. If it takes longer, the grooves were probably too shallow or the drawing was too fine. That is a normal first session. Fix the groove and continue. Do not start a second, fussier picture on the same afternoon.

Preparation

Clear a table and put down a cutting mat. You need room to turn the board without dropping it on the floor.

  1. Trim the foam to about 6 to 8 inches on the long side. Keep the corners square enough that a frame could fit later. You are not framing in this session.
  2. If the sheet is XPS about 1 inch thick, keep it. If it is regular display foam board, stop. That board is usually too thin to hold a 1/4 inch groove and still have foam underneath. The foam and supplies page is the check.
  3. Cover the working face with a double-sided adhesive sheet. Leave the top liner on. You will peel it later, one shape at a time. If you peel the whole sheet now, dust sticks to adhesive you are not ready to cover.
  4. Put a sharp blade in the craft knife. Cut one short test line in the foam margin, about 1/4 inch deep. The cut should be a slot. If the lip looks mashed or dusty, change the blade before you touch the drawing. If the knife comes through the back, the board is too thin.
  5. Press your cotton if it has a hard crease. A crease across a lobe stays visible once the adhesive grabs it. Wash and dry new cotton first if it might shrink.
  6. Set the seam ripper so the flat end is the end you will use. The forked point is for thread. A ball tip is not the tucking tool in this method. Keep both of those ends off the cloth.

What you need

Lay these out before you score the picture. None of the names below is a size you have to hunt as a special brand.

  • XPS foam about 1 inch thick, trimmed to about 6 to 8 inches.
  • A double-sided adhesive sheet large enough for the face. Double-sided tape can stand in if you keep it on the face and out of the groove. Read that choice on the supplies page before you switch.
  • A sharp craft knife, a spare blade, and a cutting mat.
  • The flat end of a seam ripper. Flat back. Not the forked point. Not a ball tip.
  • Medium-weight cotton in a few solids or calm prints. One piece for the background, one for each lobe, one for the stem. Each piece needs spare cloth past the line.
  • Paper the size of the board, a pencil, and a bit of tape to hold the paper still.

A frame can wait. You can learn the tuck before the piece is framed. Silk can wait too.

Materials

If the table is missing a board, a sheet, or a knife, the shop lists three categories: Foam and adhesive, Tools, and Starter kit.

Check the taped pattern

The drawing is a stem about a finger wide and four lobes, each at least about 1 inch across, inside a border about 1 inch from the edge of the board. Where two colors meet, there is one line. First pattern is where you draw that leaf and tape the paper to the liner. Do that before you score.

Before the knife touches the picture, check three things. The paper edges match the board. The four corners are taped, and the tape is only holding the paper, not acting as the fabric adhesive. The lines you mean to cut are dark, and the paper does not slide. If any of those is wrong, go back to the pattern page. A sheet that shifts during the score gives you two drawings.

Score the grooves

Score every dark line about 1/4 inch deep. You are cutting a groove, not slicing the board into separate pieces. Hold the knife so the blade follows the line, and pull in one steady pass. Several timid scratches make a fuzzy trench. A fuzzy trench will not grip cloth.

  • Stop while foam remains under the groove. A break through the back ruins the board.
  • At a corner, let two grooves meet. Do not dig a hole at the intersection.
  • Skip the outer edge of the foam. The bare margin is for a later frame, and for the test cut you already made.
  • If the knife crushes instead of cutting, change the blade. Do not lean harder.

Adhesive on the face

If you used a sheet, it is already under the liner. Peel the liner for one shape only, just inside that shape’s grooves. The adhesive that shows should be the field of the shape, not the channel.

Do not fill the groove with adhesive or tape. The tuck hides the edge. Adhesive in the channel gums the cloth and keeps it from sitting down. If you are using tape instead of a sheet, lay the strips on the face of this one shape after you score, and keep the strips out of the cut.

Press the middle of the cloth onto the exposed face so the color lies flat. Then work outward. A large field with nothing holding the middle will bubble, even if every edge is tucked.

Cut fabric with a margin

Rough-cut each piece so about 1/2 inch of cloth hangs past the groove on every side. That extra is the tuck. Do not scissor the piece into its finished silhouette before it is on the board. If the cloth already matches the outline, there is no edge left to push in.

Grain, print scale, and how to trim the tail are on Choosing and cutting fabric. For this session, solids or calm prints are easier to judge than a busy pattern.

The order to tuck

Work from the largest field to the narrowest piece. On this leaf that order is background, then lobes, then stem, then the border groove. The numbers on the pattern plan match that sequence.

  1. Background first. Peel only that field. Lay the cloth with the 1/2 inch margin, and press the middle. Tuck the long edges with the flat end. Use short pushes along the line. Do not saw. At each corner, tuck one side up to the meeting point, then the other. If a lump of cloth will not enter, snip into the margin toward the corner, stop before the groove, and tuck the two flaps. Do not cut a hole at the intersection.
  2. Lobes next, largest lobe first. Peel one lobe. Where the lobe meets the background, both edges share that groove. The background edge is already in. Push the lobe edge down beside it so the join reads as a line, not a ridge of loose cloth. Then tuck the lobe’s other sides.
  3. Stem last among the colored pieces. It is the narrowest, so it bunches if you feed a long bite. Push a small amount, move a finger-width, and push again. Keep the flat end in the groove. The forked point will cut the cloth or widen the slot.
  4. After each shape stays down, trim only the tail that will not fit. Trim after you have seen the tuck. A tail you cut too early cannot be tucked back.

The last side

The last side of the whole piece is the border groove: the dashed line where the background meets the bare foam margin. No second color shares that groove.

  1. Smooth the background cloth toward the border. You want about 1/2 inch past the line, not a long flap folded on top of itself.
  2. Push that edge into the border with the flat end, a short length at a time. If the cloth ridges, back the tool out, pull a little cloth up, and feed a smaller bite.
  3. Trim the leftover tail once the edge stays in the slot. Leave the foam outside the border bare. Do not wrap the cloth around the outside of the board. The margin is what a frame will cover.
  4. If a shared groove is still open because you saved it for the stem, finish that groove before you call the border the last side. The border is last only after the stem and the lobes are seated.

How to tell it is done

Set the tool down. Tip the board and look across the surface at a low angle, toward a window or a lamp.

  • No ridge sits on top of a groove. A ridge means the cloth is packed above the slot. Lift that edge with the flat end and tuck it again.
  • No adhesive shows inside the border. A shiny gap is a shape that does not cover its field, or liner peeled past the line.
  • No foam shows inside the picture. Bare foam belongs only in the margin.
  • The edge stays down when you lift the tool. You should not have to pin a shape with your finger.
  • Threads in the groove can stay. Long threads on the face should be picked off.

When those are true, stop. More poking will crush the foam. Framing is a separate step, on Finishing and framing. If a line still fails the low-angle look, use Troubleshooting before you pull the cloth off.

Mistakes that waste a board

The groove is too shallow

Fabric needs a slot, not a scratch. If the edge pops up as soon as you lift the tool, score that line a little deeper, up to about 1/4 inch, and tuck it again. Do not keep forcing the same shallow cut. Forcing packs a ridge on the line.

The foam is crushed

XPS dents. A dull knife mashes the walls, and a crushed groove will not hold cloth. Ease off, put in a sharp blade, and try a short line in the margin. Do not lean harder on the dull blade. Sharp inside corners fail first. If a section is fighting you, the drawing was too tight for a first day.

The fabric was cut to the finished shape too soon

If each piece was scissored on the drawn line, the groove has nothing to hold. You cannot invent a margin from cloth you already cut off. Cut a new piece with about 1/2 inch extra and tuck that. The figure in the cutting section is the comparison.

There is no adhesive under the cloth

The tuck hides the edge. It does not, by itself, hold the middle of a large shape. If the background bubbles when you press the center, the liner for that shape never came off, or the tape never reached the middle. Peel and press before you tuck the border again.
