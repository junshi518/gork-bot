Kinusaiga for Beginners: No‑Sew Japanese Fabric Art

A flat foam picture made by tucking fabric into shallow grooves. No store sits behind this preview.

Kinusaiga is a no-sew Japanese fabric art. You score shallow grooves in a foam board and tuck fabric edges into those lines until the colors meet as a picture. The usual result is a flat piece to frame and hang.

The word is easy to mix up with kimekomi. Both crafts tuck cloth into grooves. Kinusaiga is usually flat foam wall art. Kimekomi is often a carved wooden form. If you want a wall picture, stay here, and read Kinusaiga vs kimekomi only to keep the names apart.

This preview is for someone who just found the word and wants a first piece. It is not a history essay and not a pattern download. When a name is required, the only credit used here is: Japanese artist, often credited as Maeno Setsu (also seen as Maeno Takashi) in the 1980s. Sources disagree, and this site does not pick one name as fact. There are no original photos and no licensed patterns. This preview does not offer a free pattern library.

Where a first piece starts

Use a board about 6 to 8 inches, large shapes, and few sharp corners. A fine drawing hides whether the trouble is the design or the tuck. The beginners guide walks that first session and the mistakes that waste a board: a shallow groove, no adhesive, fabric trimmed before it is tucked, and a hand that crushes the foam.

What you need on the table

Plan on about 1-inch XPS foam, a double-sided adhesive sheet, and a score about 1/4 inch deep. Regular display foam board is usually too thin to hold a tuck. Add a sharp craft knife, a blunt tucking tool such as the flat back of a seam ripper, and medium-weight cotton. Silk is not required. The foam and supplies guide compares those choices. The figure here is a schematic, not a photo. Hands-on photos are not in this preview yet.

Kit or loose materials

A complete kit holds a board, a way to keep fabric in place, cloth for the design, and ideally a frame and a tucking tool, when the box matches that list. Loose foam and adhesive fit better if quilting cotton and a frame are already at home. No price is listed.
